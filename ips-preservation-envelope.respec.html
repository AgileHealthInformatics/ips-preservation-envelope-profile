This profile specifies how a healthcare organisation can issue a patient summary as two representations of one clinical statement: a preservation envelope, a PDF/A-3 document whose human-readable pages are generated from, and which embeds, the exact octets of a structured clinical document; and an exchange projection, those same octets registered and exchanged as a document in their own right. It defines rules for the structured source, provenance and assurance, rendition fidelity, the envelope, the issuance lifecycle and preservation, together with a content binding to HL7 FHIR International Patient Summary 2.0.1 and an exchange binding to IHE XDS.b, XDS Metadata Update, MHD, sIPS, XCA and XCPD. Every rule is identified, states how it is verified, and is published as a machine-readable rule catalogue with a conformance kit of test vectors and live scenarios.

The central design rule is that the preservation container is not the exchange protocol.

This is an unofficial draft prepared by an individual. It is not a standard and has not been endorsed by HL7 International, IHE International, ISO, the European Commission or any Member State. It must not be cited as evidence of conformance with Regulation (EU) 2025/327 [[EHDS]], Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2083 [[EU-2026-2083]], the European electronic health record exchange format, MyHealth@EU, or any HL7 or IHE specification.

Version 0.5.0 responds to a reassessment of version 0.4.0 [[REVIEW-040]]. It changes no part of the architecture. It completes the obligation model, so that every normative obligation now carries exactly one requirement keyword or declares its acceptance conditions, each with one; makes withdrawal an explicit event of the issuer and stops inferring it from XDS deprecation and a missing replacement, with a modifier extension in place of the 0.4.0 extension in MHD; removes the use of Organization.partOf as a substitute for provenance; defines the attested content digest as a complete algorithm with cross-language vectors; states when each of the five timestamps applies; and makes the verification route of every mandatory obligation explicit. records how each item was handled and traces every changed obligation to its reason and its tests.

Introduction

The problem

Records management needs a durable, human-intelligible artefact that can be retained, audited and produced years later exactly as it was issued. Interoperability needs a computable representation that other systems, including systems in other jurisdictions, can process without human interpretation. A common failure is to satisfy one need with an artefact designed for the other. A second is to keep both but generate them independently, so that the pages and the data can disagree. A third, which this version addresses directly, is a document that is technically well-formed, cryptographically intact and correctly exchanged, but whose visible meaning or claimed clinical authority has not been adequately assured.

The pattern

  1. One structured source. The clinical document is composed, validated and serialised once. Those octets are fixed for the life of the issuance.
  2. One-way rendition under a fidelity contract. The pages are generated only from the structured source, must show everything the contract requires in its canonical form and context, and are packaged with the source.
  3. Honest assurance. An issuance states whether it is a machine-generated snapshot or an attested clinical document, and claims attestation only with evidence of an attestation action.
  4. One publication unit, two registry entries. The structured source and the envelope are registered together, with distinct format codes. The envelope is recorded as a transformation of the source, and replacement follows the source.
  5. Preservation stays home. Only the computable document crosses a community boundary, carrying the provenance a receiver needs. Receivers render it for their own readers and may keep a custody copy; the issuer's envelope and its preservation events remain the record of issuance.

Normative obligations

Version 0.3.0 inferred a rule's strength from the first requirement keyword in its text, which misclassified rules that combine obligations of different strength. From 0.4.0 every obligation is declared explicitly. A rule whose text contains a single obligation is that obligation, identified by the rule identifier (for example ENV-01). A rule with several obligations marks each one, identified as the rule identifier followed by a letter (for example PRES-05.a). Each obligation declares:

  • its requirement level: MUST or MUST NOT (mandatory); SHOULD or SHOULD NOT (advisory). This version uses no permissive keyword;
  • the conformance classes and option it applies to, inherited from its rule unless stated; an option may be negated (not:PP: applies when PP is not claimed);
  • its verification methods (), its tests, and its evidence requirement.

From 0.5.0 an obligation contains exactly one requirement keyword. Where several conditions must hold together for one indivisible requirement, the obligation declares them as acceptance conditions, numbered and identified as the obligation identifier followed by a solidus and the number (for example CHK-07/2); each condition contains one keyword, all conditions of an obligation are of the same strength (mandatory or advisory), and the obligation passes only when every condition holds. Independent requirements are separate obligations, because they are separately testable. A mandatory obligation that has no test of its own declares its verification route: a declaration in the claim, a declared inspection, or a recorded gap in the conformance kit ().

The build rejects a rule whose text contains a requirement keyword outside a declared obligation, an obligation with more than one keyword and no acceptance conditions, a condition with other than one keyword, an obligation whose keywords disagree with its declared level, a mandatory obligation with neither a test nor a verification route, and a test reference that names no vector or scenario. The rule catalogue publishes the obligations with their conditions and routes; the Checker evaluates obligations, not rule text (CHK-07, CHK-09). Splitting rules into obligations in 0.5.0 replaced some obligation identifiers: a rule that had one obligation and now has several (for example SRC-04, now SRC-04.a to SRC-04.c) no longer has an obligation with the bare rule identifier. Where an existing lettered obligation was split, it keeps its letter for the part that stays and the part that moves takes the next free letter (for example PROV-02.a and PROV-02.d).

Part I — Core preservation profile

These rules do not depend on a particular document format or sharing infrastructure. Where a rule needs a concrete element, it defers to the content or exchange binding.

Structured source

The [=Issuer=] MUST serialise the [=structured source=] exactly once, and validation, embedding, registration and exchange use those octets. Those octets MUST NOT be re-serialised, reformatted or canonicalised after [=issuance=].

Before [=issuance=], the [=Issuer=] MUST validate the [=structured source=] with the validator the content binding names; it MUST NOT issue a source that produces validation errors; it MUST retain the validator name and version, the package versions and the outcome in the [=issuance record=].

The [=document identifier=] MUST be globally unique: an identifier, once assigned, is never assigned again. Every [=issuance=] MUST carry a [=series identifier=]. Successive issuances that replace one another MUST share their [=series identifier=].

An [=issuance=] that replaces an earlier one MUST carry, in the [=structured source=], a machine-readable reference to the replaced [=document identifier=], as the content binding defines.

Provenance and assurance

Version 0.2.0 required every issuance to carry a legal attester, and the demonstrator met that requirement by recording one automatically. A generated attester identifier is not evidence that anyone attested anything. This version separates two assurance levels and makes the stronger one an option with an evidence obligation.

The [=structured source=] MUST identify the [=author=] and the [=custodian=] as distinct roles, even when one party holds both. The [=issuance record=] MUST record the [=renderer=], the assurance level (PROV-05) and the [=source organisation=] as PROV-10 establishes it, or that it is unavailable.

An [=attested issuance=] MUST carry, in the [=structured source=], an attestation by the person or organisation that takes responsibility for its content, with the attestation mode and time. Its [=issuance record=] MUST hold evidence of the attestation action: the attester's identity, the time, the method, the statement made, and the [=attested content digest=] of the content reviewed. The [=rendition=] MUST show the [=author=], the [=custodian=], the document status, the [=document identifier=] and the assurance statement of PROV-05. For an [=attested issuance=] the [=rendition=] MUST also show the [=attester=] with the mode and time of attestation.

An [=issuance=] for which the evidence of PROV-02 does not exist MUST NOT claim attestation in the [=structured source=], the exchange metadata, the [=issuance record=] or the [=rendition=]. It is a [=preserved snapshot=].

The assurance level of each [=issuance=], [=preserved snapshot=] or [=attested issuance=], MUST be recorded in the [=issuance record=] and stated in the [=rendition=] with the wording the content binding defines.

Document-level provenance survives exchange: the [=structured source=] MUST carry the [=author=], the [=custodian=] (the issuing organisation), the clinical content time, the document status, the [=document identifier=] and [=series identifier=], any replaced [=document identifier=] and, for an [=attested issuance=], the [=attester=] and attestation time, so that a [=Receiver=] can establish them without the [=preservation envelope=] or the [=issuance record=]. assigns every provenance attribute to the structured source, the sharing metadata or the issuance record.

Where source systems record the provenance of individual clinical statements (for example who recorded, asserted or performed them), the [=Issuer=] SHOULD carry it in the [=structured source=]. The document-level [=author=] MUST NOT be presented, by an Issuer or a Receiver, as the author of each individual clinical statement. This profile does not require source systems to expose identical provenance structures.

An [=attested issuance=] MUST be produced by the sequence of : the attester reviews a validated draft identified by its [=attested content digest=], the attestation is recorded against that digest, and only then is the final [=structured source=], carrying the attestation, created, validated, rendered, packaged, preserved and published. An issuance whose clinical content differs from the content attested MUST NOT be published as attested until the changed content has itself been attested.

The clinical content time, the issuance time, the attestation time, the document-sharing submission time and the reference-indexing time are distinct times of distinct events. Each of them MUST be carried, once the event it records has occurred, in the element assigns to it: the clinical content time and the issuance time for every [=issuance=]; the attestation time for an [=attested issuance=] only; the submission time once the [=publication unit=] is registered; the reference-indexing time once a document reference is created. The element of one of these times MUST NOT carry another of them. The element of a time whose event has not occurred (for example the attestation time of a [=preserved snapshot=]) MUST NOT be populated. A [=rendition=] MUST show the clinical content time and the issuance time as separately labelled values.

The [=source organisation=] is the organisation from whose records clinical content was taken. Where the structured source states which organisation supplied clinical content, it MUST do so by explicit provenance: a Provenance resource whose target is the entry concerned, or an element of the entry that identifies the source, such as informationSource, recorder or asserter. An organisational relationship, such as Organization.partOf, MUST NOT be used to state or imply that an organisation supplied clinical content. Where the source of clinical content is not available as explicit provenance, the [=issuance record=] MUST state that source-level provenance is unavailable, rather than assert a source.

Attestation sequence

This sequence is normative for the [=Attested Issuance option=] (PROV-08). It is a design decision of this profile, not a workflow prescribed by HL7.

  1. Compose the draft clinical content.
  2. Validate the draft and compute its [=attested content digest=].
  3. Record the accountable attestation against that digest: attester, time, method, statement.
  4. Create the final [=structured source=] by adding the attestation to the reviewed draft and nothing else that the digest covers (IPS-07, algorithm ACD-1 of ); validate it and confirm its attested content digest equals the one attested.
  5. Render, package, validate and preserve the [=preservation envelope=].
  6. Register and publish the [=publication unit=] atomically.

The [=issuance record=] keeps the attested content digest, the attestation evidence, the digest of the final structured source and the digest of the envelope. A reviewer can confirm what was approved by recomputing the attested content digest from the issued source and comparing it with the evidence.

Rendition fidelity

Fidelity has four facets, each a separate rule so that a [=Checker=] can report them separately: coverage (REN-02), accuracy (REN-10), context (REN-11) and absence of unsupported assertions (REN-08), with recursion over the document structure (REN-12).

A text-based check can show that every required element is visible, in its canonical form, in the right section and entry, and that no code from elsewhere appears. It cannot establish complete clinical semantic equivalence between the pages and the data, nor clinical safety (REN-13). FHIR R4 requires a section narrative to contain enough information to be clinically safe for a human reader, without necessarily repeating every structured attribute [[FHIR-NARRATIVE]]; this profile's contract is stricter for the elements it lists and relies on the deployment's clinical risk management for the rest.

A [=rendition=] MUST NOT introduce any clinical statement from an input other than the [=structured source=]; its other inputs are [=presentation resources=] only.

Coverage: every element that marks R (required visible) or E (declared equivalent) and that is populated in the [=structured source=] MUST be visible in the [=rendition=].

Traceability. Every clinical assertion shown within a section of a [=rendition=] MUST be traceable to the [=structured source=], either to an entry of that section or to that section's narrative in the source. A coded concept that the section's entries and source narrative do not carry is not traceable. A clinical assertion MUST NOT be shown in a way that misattributes it to another entry or contradicts the source.

Accuracy: each element covered by REN-02 MUST be shown in the [=canonical text form=] the content binding defines for it, or in the equivalent representation the coverage matrix declares, so that a change in the value changes the visible text.

Context. Each element MUST be shown within the presentation of the entry it belongs to (a row or block beginning with that entry's first contract element). The presentation of an entry MUST be within the presentation of its own section. Qualifiers such as status, verification status, severity, negation, dosage and dates MUST NOT be detached from the statement they qualify.

Completeness is recursive: REN-02 to REN-11 apply to every section at every depth. A section with nested sections MUST show its title and its nested sections. A section without entries or nested sections MUST show its empty reason or, if it has none, its narrative, which is then clinical content.

A fidelity result obtained by automated checking MUST NOT be presented as establishing complete clinical semantic equivalence or clinical safety. A [=Checker=] report MUST state this limitation.

Where the [=structured source=] populates an element that marks N (not covered by automated verification), the [=rendition=] MUST show it, and its fidelity MUST be established by the separate assurance declared in the conformance claim.

The pages of a [=preservation envelope=] MUST show the [=document identifier=]. They MUST state that the structured source is embedded in the file.

Rendition MUST be deterministic: the same [=structured source=], renderer version, language and versions of all [=presentation resources=] produce the same visible content. Byte-identical output SHOULD be achieved.

The renderer name and version MUST be recorded in the envelope metadata (XMP xmp:CreatorTool and the document information Creator entry) and in the [=issuance record=].

Section narratives carried in the [=structured source=] SHOULD be generated by the same process and from the same data as the pages.

The language of the [=rendition=] MUST be declared in the [=structured source=] as the content binding defines and SHOULD be declared in the envelope's XMP dc:language.

Terminology designations used in a [=rendition=] are clinical content. Their source and version MUST be recorded with the [=rendition=] (in the [=issuance record=] or the [=custody copy=]). They MUST be distinguished from translations of fixed labels, which carry no clinical meaning.

Preservation envelope

These rules can be checked from the file alone, which is why they define the [=Envelope=] conformance class.

The [=preservation envelope=] MUST conform to PDF/A-3 [[ISO-19005-3]] at conformance level b or stricter. Its XMP metadata MUST declare pdfaid:part = 3 and the conformance level.

The [=structured source=] MUST be embedded as a file whose file specification has /AFRelationship /Source, listed in the document catalog's /AF array and in the EmbeddedFiles name tree.

The embedded file stream MUST declare the media type of the [=structured source=] in /Subtype. It MUST carry /Params with /ModDate. /Params SHOULD carry /Size.

The file specification MUST carry /F and /UF and SHOULD carry /Desc.

The decoded octets of the embedded file MUST be identical to the [=structured source=].

The envelope MUST contain exactly one associated file with relationship Source. Other embedded files SHOULD NOT be present; any that are MUST be reported by a [=Checker=].

The envelope MUST NOT be encrypted. The envelope MUST NOT contain JavaScript, open actions or launch actions.

Before [=issuance=], the [=Issuer=] MUST validate the envelope with an independent PDF/A validator such as veraPDF [[VERAPDF]]; it MUST NOT treat successful generation as evidence of conformance; it MUST retain the validator's report.

The XMP metadata SHOULD carry the [=document identifier=] in dc:identifier.

All text in the pages SHOULD have a Unicode mapping (a ToUnicode map, or a simple font with a standard encoding), so that REN-02 can be verified from the page text.

An issued envelope MUST NOT be modified, including by incremental update. A correction MUST be made by a new [=issuance=].

Issuance and lifecycle

For each [=issuance=] the [=Issuer=] MUST keep an [=issuance record=] containing at least: the [=document identifier=], [=series identifier=] and version; the issue time; the assurance level and, for an [=attested issuance=], the attestation evidence of PROV-02; the provenance of PROV-01; SHA-256 [[FIPS-180-4]] digests of the envelope and of the [=structured source=]; the renderer and the versions of all [=presentation resources=]; the binding versions; the validation evidence of SRC-04 and ENV-08; and, for a replacement, the replaced identifier and the reason (LIF-07). Later lifecycle changes are recorded as [=preservation events=], not by editing the record.

The [=structured source=] and the envelope of an [=issuance=] MUST be immutable. Replacement MUST be made by a new [=issuance=] with a new [=document identifier=].

A replaced or withdrawn [=issuance=] MUST remain retrievable, subject to retention policy. It MUST still pass the integrity checks of XCH-03.

A [=returned instance=] from an [=on-demand service=] MUST NOT be presented as an [=issuance=]. It MUST be distinguishable from an [=issuance=] by consumers.

A [=stable snapshot=], whether or not it is retained and registered, MUST NOT be treated as an [=issuance=] unless it passes the gate of LIF-05. When a [=stable snapshot=] is registered it MUST be linked to its [=on-demand service=] as the exchange binding defines.

An [=issuance=] MUST NOT be published unless SRC-04, PROV-04, REN-02, REN-08, REN-10, REN-11, REN-12, ENV-05 and ENV-08 have been satisfied for it and, when it is presented as an [=attested issuance=], PROV-02.

The [=Issuer=] MUST distinguish, and record: replacement for update (the earlier issuance was correct when issued); replacement for correction (the earlier issuance contained an error); and withdrawal (an issuance entered in error, withdrawn without a replacement). These are the transitions of .

A [=publication unit=] MUST become visible to consumers atomically. Where the infrastructure cannot guarantee this, the [=Publisher=] MUST NOT expose an incomplete unit: it stages the unit and reconciles or rolls back a partial failure. The same applies to the metadata changes of a lifecycle transition.

Lifecycle transitions follow . A transition not listed there MUST NOT be performed. Each transition MUST make exactly the metadata changes the table lists.

Historical retrievability is distinct from currency. An [=issuance=] in any state other than issued MUST remain retrievable by its identifiers for the retention period; MUST NOT be returned as the [=current summary=]; MUST be represented in each layer as specifies for its state.

After a replacement for correction or a withdrawal, the [=Issuer=] MUST record a notification-required [=preservation event=] naming the known recipients of the earlier issuance (those to which its projection is known to have been disclosed) and SHOULD notify them, because the earlier issuance may already have informed care.

Withdrawal and replacement are different events (LIF-07), and a sharing layer's status does not by itself say which occurred. A withdrawn [=issuance=] MUST NOT be presented in any layer as a replacement. Where defines an explicit representation of a lifecycle state for a layer, that representation MUST be provided in that layer. A consumer derives what it presents about an issuance that is not current only from explicit representations, as follows: it MUST present the issuance as replaced only when a replacing issuance is identified (an RPLC association from it or a relatesTo replaces that targets it); it MUST present the issuance as withdrawn only when an explicit withdrawal representation of is present; in every other case it MUST present the issuance as no longer current, reason unknown.

Withdrawal is an event of the [=Issuer=], not a registry status. A withdrawal MUST be performed as an explicit lifecycle action of the [=Issuer=] and recorded as a withdrawal [=preservation event=] stating the withdrawn [=document identifier=], the reason, the party that authorised the withdrawal and the time. The lifecycle state of an [=issuance=] MUST be determined from the [=Issuer=]'s lifecycle events, which are kept independently of the availability status and associations of any registry. The reason an issuance is no longer current MUST NOT be inferred from an XDS availabilityStatus of Deprecated or from the absence of an RPLC association: deprecation in XDS does not record why an entry was deprecated, and the absence of an association is not evidence of a withdrawal.

Preservation

PDF/A conformance and digests establish properties of an artefact; they are not a preservation capability. These rules define the information an [=Issuer=] supplies, the evidence model for [=preservation events=] and what a [=Repository=] must do, without specifying a whole archive (for which see [[ISO-14721]]).

The [=Issuer=] MUST supply, with each [=issuance=], the representation information needed to interpret it later: the content binding and package versions, FHIR version, PDF/A part and level, renderer version, and the versions of terminologies and [=presentation resources=] used.

Fixity MUST be established with SHA-256 digests recorded independently of transport metadata. A transport hash that uses a weaker algorithm (such as the SHA-1 hash of an XDS DocumentEntry) MUST NOT be relied on to detect deliberate alteration.

[=Preservation events=] MUST be recorded separately from the issued artefacts. Recording a [=preservation event=] MUST NOT modify an issued artefact. Each event MUST have at least: an event identifier; a type (issuance, validation, attestation, replacement, correction, withdrawal, notification-required or fixity-check); a timestamp; the responsible agent; the affected artefact; the digest concerned, where there is one; the outcome; and links to supporting evidence.

A deployment MUST declare which party performs periodic fixity checking, storage redundancy, recovery and format migration for issued artefacts, and at what frequency.

With the [=Protected Preservation option=], the [=issuance record=] and the [=preservation events=] MUST be protected against undetected alteration by an independent mechanism (an append-only log whose chain head is held by a party independent of the Repository, an electronic seal, or a trusted timestamp). With that option, the digest of each [=issuance record=] MUST be recorded in its issuance event. Without that option, the issuance record and events SHOULD be protected in the same way.

A [=Repository=] checks fixity without relying on transport metadata. It MUST compare each stored artefact with the SHA-256 digest recorded at [=issuance=]. It MUST record every check, including failures, as a [=preservation event=]. It MUST NOT alter or remove earlier events when doing so.

Attested content digest

This section is normative (IPS-07). It defines algorithm ACD-1, which computes the [=attested content digest=] of an IPS Bundle. The digest is the same for a reviewed draft and for the issued structured source that adds the attestation to it, and it differs whenever clinical content differs. Version 0.4.0 described the algorithm in one sentence, which left number representation, string escaping and the entries to remove open to interpretation; two implementations could disagree.

Input: the octets of a FHIR JSON document Bundle. Output: 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, or rejection of the input.

  1. Parse. Decode the octets as UTF-8 and parse them as JSON [[RFC8259]]. Reject the input if it is not valid UTF-8, begins with a byte order mark, is not JSON, contains an object with two members of the same name, or contains a string that is not a sequence of Unicode scalar values (an unpaired surrogate, whether written literally or as an escape). Keep each number as the exact sequence of characters that represents it in the input; do not convert it to a binary value. FHIR carries the precision of a decimal in its written form, so 5.0 and 5 are different content.
  2. Remove the issuance members. Reject the input unless it is an object whose resourceType is Bundle and whose first entry has a resource whose resourceType is Composition. Remove the Bundle members id, identifier and timestamp.
  3. Remove the attestation. In that Composition, collect into a set P every string found at attester[*].party.reference, then remove the member attester.
  4. Remove the entries only the attestation uses. Let C be the entries, other than the first, whose fullUrl is in P. A reference is the string value of any object member named reference, at any depth. Let R be the set of references in the Bundle as it stands after step 3, with the entries of C left out. Remove each entry of C whose fullUrl is not in R. The remaining entries keep their order. Comparison is exact string equality; references are not resolved, so an attester whose party reference is not the fullUrl of an entry removes no entry.
  5. Serialise. Write the result as the JSON Canonicalization Scheme does [[RFC8785]], with one exception. Object members are sorted by name, comparing names as sequences of UTF-16 code units; there is no white space; arrays keep their order; true, false and null are written as such. In strings, quotation mark and reverse solidus are escaped with a reverse solidus; U+0008, U+0009, U+000A, U+000C and U+000D are written \b, \t, \n, \f and \r; other characters below U+0020 are written \u followed by four lowercase hexadecimal digits; every other character, including the solidus, non-ASCII characters and characters outside the Basic Multilingual Plane, is written as itself. Exception: each number is written as the character sequence kept in step 1, not in the ECMAScript form that RFC 8785 prescribes, which would write 5.0 as 5. Encode the result as UTF-8.
  6. Digest. The [=attested content digest=] is the SHA-256 [[FIPS-180-4]] digest of those octets, written as lowercase hexadecimal.

FHIR R4 also describes a canonical JSON form, for use with signatures [[FHIR-JSON]]. ACD-1 does not depend on it: the exclusions above are specific to attestation, and the algorithm is complete in itself so that implementations in different languages agree byte for byte. ACD-1 is not a substitute for the issued octets: the [=structured source=] is still serialised once and never re-serialised (SRC-02).

Part III — Exchange binding: IHE document sharing

This binding uses XDS.b, XCA and XCPD as specified in ITI TF Revision 20.2 [[IHE-ITI-TF]], ITI-57 as specified in the XDS Metadata Update supplement Revision 1.14 [[IHE-XDS-MU]], MHD 4.2.4 [[IHE-MHD]] and sIPS 1.0.0 [[IHE-SIPS]], as fixed by .

Registration and metadata

Each [=issuance=] MUST be registered as two DocumentEntries, one for the [=exchange projection=] and one for the [=preservation envelope=], together with their associations, in a single Provide and Register Document Set-b (ITI-41) SubmissionSet.

The envelope DocumentEntry MUST have mimeType application/pdf and an [=envelope format code=]. The envelope DocumentEntry MUST NOT carry the [=sIPS format code=].

The projection DocumentEntry MUST have mimeType application/fhir+json and formatCode equal to the [=sIPS format code=]: code http://hl7.org/fhir/uv/ips/StructureDefinition/Bundle-uv-ips, coding scheme urn:ietf:rfc:3986.

The envelope DocumentEntry MUST be associated to the projection DocumentEntry by an XFRM association whose sourceObject is the envelope and whose targetObject is the projection, because the envelope is rendered from the [=structured source=].

A replacing projection MUST be associated to the replaced projection by an RPLC association. The replaced envelope is then deprecated as a transformation of the replaced document, as ITI TF-3 requires. The new envelope MUST be associated to the new projection by XFRM (MET-04).

The hash and size of each DocumentEntry MUST correspond to the stored octets. The SHA-256 digests of LIF-01 MUST be kept separately (PRES-02).

Both DocumentEntries of an [=issuance=] MUST have the same patientId, creationTime, classCode, typeCode, confidentialityCode and languageCode.

Each DocumentEntry MUST be presented as a DocumentReference conforming to MHD 4.2.4 whose id is assigned by the server, without requiring clients to derive it from, or equate it with, the entryUUID. content.format MUST equal the format code. status, relatesTo and the extensions of MUST follow that mapping. relatesTo MUST reference its targets by their server-assigned ids. Times MUST follow .

A query restricted to the [=sIPS format code=] MUST NOT return an envelope DocumentEntry.

Identifiers MUST be mapped as in . The two DocumentEntries of an [=issuance=] MUST have different uniqueId values, because their octets differ. Where a FHIR view is offered: the entryUUID MUST be carried only as the entryUUID identifier slice and the uniqueId as masterIdentifier (and, if present, the uniqueId slice), each with its MHD identifier type; both MUST be searchable with the identifier search parameter.

A withdrawal (LIF-07) MUST change the availabilityStatus of both DocumentEntries from Approved to Deprecated, without a replacement, in one Update Document Set (ITI-57) carrying an UpdateAvailabilityStatus association per entry, with sourceObject the SubmissionSet, targetObject the DocumentEntry and slots OriginalStatus and NewStatus [[IHE-XDS-MU]]. The Responder MUST apply both changes or neither. The availability change records that the entries are no longer current; it does not record why (LIF-13).

An [=on-demand service=] MUST be represented as sIPS defines for on-demand documents. A [=stable snapshot=] MUST be linked to its [=on-demand service=] as sIPS defines. The [=envelope format code=] MUST NOT be used for either of them.

legalAuthenticator MUST be populated only for an [=attested issuance=] whose [=attester=] is a person, and only from that attester. A FHIR view MUST map legalAuthenticator to DocumentReference.authenticator. An attestation by an organisation, or by a person who cannot be expressed as an XCN within the author institution, is carried only in the [=structured source=] and the [=issuance record=]; a person MUST NOT be invented to populate legalAuthenticator. When the [=author=] is software, authorPerson MUST identify the software as an XCN software agent, and authorRole MUST NOT be used to describe it, because authorRole states the author's role in the documented act.

Where the registry supports extra metadata and the update of DocumentEntry metadata [[IHE-XDS-MU]], the withdrawal's Update Document Set SHOULD also set, on both DocumentEntries, the extra-metadata slot urn:oid:2.25.11064312502901710892401295388462879468.2 with the single value withdrawn, taken from the withdrawal event of LIF-13. Interoperability of this slot across registries and gateways is untested (issue IR-14); its absence never means that an entry was not withdrawn (LIF-12.c).

Retrieval and exchange

A consumer MUST be able to obtain the [=exchange projection=] without retrieving or parsing the envelope.

The octets returned for the [=exchange projection=] MUST be identical to the embedded [=structured source=]: a Responder does not re-serialise them. In a FHIR Binary, the base64 content MUST decode to those octets.

A consumer that relies on an envelope SHOULD verify the envelope SHA-256 and size, the presence and digest of the Source file, equality of the embedded octets with the projection, the [=document identifier=] and REN-02.

Cross-community exchange

This version binds patient discovery to XCPD (ITI-55) and query and retrieve to XCA (ITI-38, ITI-39). See also the open issue on a FHIR-based binding in .

A [=Responding Gateway=] MUST NOT release a [=preservation envelope=] to another community; the [=exchange projection=] is the only representation that crosses the community boundary.

A [=Responding Gateway=] MUST NOT release to another community the content of an [=issuance=] that is not the [=current summary=], whether it was replaced or withdrawn. It SHOULD answer a query by identifier for such an issuance with its metadata, its availability status, the RPLC associations that target it and any explicit lifecycle representation that defines for the registry, so that a receiver that holds it can learn that it is no longer current and, where the metadata says so, why.

In patient discovery a [=Responding Gateway=] MUST NOT disclose a patient identifier unless exactly one candidate matches.

Before clinical use, a [=Receiver=] MUST verify the received octets against the advertised hash and size, that the media type and format code identify the content binding's format, that the subject is the patient for whom the documents were requested, and that the document is current; it MUST establish from the received [=structured source=] alone the document-level provenance of PROV-06.

A [=Receiver=] MUST NOT modify the received octets; localisation operates on a derived view.

In a [=rendition=] in another language, a coded concept with no available designation MUST be shown in its original form with an explicit marker; free text MUST NOT be silently machine-translated.

A [=Receiver=]'s [=rendition=] MUST state the [=home community=], the [=document identifier=] and the time of retrieval. It MUST NOT present itself as the issuer's record.

A [=Receiver=] SHOULD keep a [=custody copy=] as a PDF/A-3 file whose Source file is the received octets and whose pages are the [=rendition=] shown. A [=custody copy=] MUST NOT be registered or presented as an [=issuance=] of the [=home community=].

When a [=Receiver=] presents a previously received [=issuance=] that it has learned is no longer current, the presentation MUST say that it is no longer current; it MUST state whether the issuance was replaced, was withdrawn, or is no longer current for a reason the Receiver cannot determine, decided as LIF-12.c requires.

Relationship to the European Health Data Space

Regulation (EU) 2025/327 [[EHDS]] establishes rights of access to electronic health data and MyHealth@EU for cross-border exchange. Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2083 on MyHealth@EU [[EU-2026-2083]] was adopted on 18 September 2026 and published on 21 September 2026. It enters into force on 11 October 2026 and applies from 26 March 2027, with cross-border exchange of patient summaries from 26 March 2029. It sets the technical, organisational, security and data-protection framework for MyHealth@EU, including conditions and compliance checks for national contact points.

This profile is not a MyHealth@EU specification. Being able to exchange an IPS through IHE transactions does not establish conformance with the technical specifications applicable under that framework, which are governed through its own arrangements. What the profile contributes is an issuer-side discipline: preserve what was issued, say honestly how it was assured, exchange the computable document as itself, and keep evidence. Under the layered structure, a content binding to whatever format is mandated for MyHealth@EU exchange could be added without changing the core rules.
